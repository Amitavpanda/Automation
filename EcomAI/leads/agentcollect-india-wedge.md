## 3. White space (honest)
Nobody holds native books + credit-risk + B2B storefront + WhatsApp-first + AI + India legal escalation on a single source-of-truth ledger for Indian SME wholesale. Moat = ledger -> collections -> credit data flywheel. CredFlow has an invoice lake and an NBFC. Recordent has a registry. Own the ledger AND the collection outcome.

## 5. India translation
Size: Rs 7.4-8.1 lakh crore (~$88-97B) delayed MSME payments. India's built-in "attorney letter":
- MSMED Act 15/16: 45-day payment rule; interest = 3x bank rate, compounded monthly (about 16.5-19.5% today); MSEFC arbitration with enforceable awards; appeal requires 75% deposit.
- Section 43B(h) IT Act: buyer loses tax deduction on unpaid MSME invoices. Auditors force settlement.
- Samadhaan portal moving to new MSME ODR portal.
- Advocate/legal demand notice costs Rs 1-3k. This is the Indian equivalent of AgentCollect's attorney email at a fraction of the price.

EcomAI Collect (the AgentCollect-India wedge):
- Pull AR from EcomAI ledger or Tally import.
- WhatsApp-first dunning (soft to firm to advocate to ODR), UPI payment links, AI negotiation of payment plans.
- One-click "legal pack": statement + MSMED demand letter + 3x interest calc + 43B(h) warning + ODR filing.
- Success-fee pricing (8-15% of recovered) — the SaaS stays subscription, the service is pay-on-recovery.
- Ticket focus Rs 50k-5L (wholesaler to retailer). Recovery money lands in days, not quarters.
- Data flywheel: every collection improves credit-risk model; later enables TReDS/discounting revenue share and lender partnerships.

Productize vs SaaS: do both but in sequence. SaaS subscription = books + storefront + reminders (TAGHills pays monthly). EcomAI Collect = separate service line, success-fee, run as "AI collection agency on your ledger data." The service is the wedge that makes the SaaS stick and the data flywheel turn.
